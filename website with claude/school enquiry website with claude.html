How admissions works

Six steps, roughly four months from first visit to enrollment. Families can start at any point in the fall.

01

Inquire

Tell us a little about your child and the grade you're applying for. This opens your family's file and gets you on the mailing list for tour dates and info sessions.

5 minutes, anytime
02

Visit campus

Walk the grounds, sit in on a class, and meet the division head for your child's grade. Group tours run weekly; private visits are available for working families.

September – December
03

Submit the application

Complete the family form, upload the two most recent report cards, and share a teacher recommendation. There's no essay for students below grade 6.

Due January 15
04

Student visit & conversation

Applicants spend a half day with a class their age. For grades 6 and up, this includes a short, unscored conversation with an admissions teacher — not a test.

January – February
05

Decision

The admissions committee reviews every file together. Families hear back by mail and email, with financial aid decisions released on the same day.

Mid-March
06

Enroll

Return the enrollment contract and deposit, then join us for New Family Day in August — a chance to meet classmates and teachers before the first day.

April 1 deadline

Four divisions, one campus

Students stay on the same grounds from age five through graduation, which means older students mentor younger ones and teachers watch a child grow across a decade, not a single year.

Grades 9–12

Upper School

Students choose from 26 electives and design a senior capstone project with a faculty advisor. Class sizes stay under 16, and every student presents original research before graduation. Roughly a third of the week is unscheduled, protected time for independent work.

Ages 5–6

Early Years

Two full-time teachers per classroom of 16, with most mornings spent outdoors regardless of weather.

Grades 1–5

Lower School

Reading, math, and one long project block each week — this year's third grade is mapping the creek that runs behind the science building.

Grades 6–8

Middle School

Students switch classrooms for the first time and pick up a second language, an instrument, and a standing weekly debate.
